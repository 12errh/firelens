The question

A lunar habitat runs at 30% oxygen or more. Earth runs at 21%.

More oxygen means easier ignition and faster flame spread. NASA has decades of microgravity combustion research — BASS, FLEX, ACME, SAFFIRE, SAME — and almost none of it was run at lunar oxygen levels.

The archive is large

24 Combustion Science investigations, 22 experimental summary tables, 2,870 normalized rows, 352 publications, 109 reports and science documents.

The archive is uneven

Only 12 of 22 tables report oxygen. Pressure is missing from most solid-material campaigns. Missing stays missing — never estimated.

The archive has a hole

Every tabulated combustion test in PSI ran in microgravity or at 1 g. Zero ran at lunar or Mars gravity.

“The intermediate oxygen concentration used on the Moon will likely be 30% or greater… Compared to Earth’s atmosphere, that’s more than a 40% increase in the oxygen concentration making materials much more flammable.”

The product

An evidence workbench, not a chatbot.

The hero is an isometric combustion bench: turn the gravity knob and the flame falls from a 1 g teardrop to a microgravity sphere.

Evidence map

Every summary table plotted by reported oxygen and gravity. The empty lane is the finding, so it is drawn, not hidden.

Investigation explorer

24 records filtered by gravity class, material phase and data availability, with the source table behind every number.

Grounded assistant

Retrieves 2,583 passages from 91 downloaded NASA reports, quotes them verbatim with a page number, and says “not reported” when the snapshot is silent.

100% citation accuracy and numeric grounding

The numeric guard accepts a percentage only if it appears in the normalized tables or in a cited passage. The deterministic path needs no API key, so the assistant works even with the model offline.

Evidence classes and honest limits

Four kinds of support. Only one is a measurement.

Direct — 12 points

Microgravity measurements from flight experiments. Real, but nearly all sit at Earth-like oxygen.

1 g comparison — 1 point

SAFFIRE-II reports one matched normal-gravity column. It bounds gravity sensitivity for one material set, nothing more.

Analog only — 1 record

E-FIELD mimics buoyancy-free behavior with electric fields. Useful, explicitly not a gravity measurement.

Absent — 0 datasets

Lunar and Mars gravity. The gap is in the archive, not only in our interface.

Who this is for

  • Fire-safety engineers sizing a materials envelope before a design review.
  • Mission designers comparing a spacecraft cabin with a lunar habitat.
  • Researchers finding which PSI record answers their question — and which does not.
  • Students who need the real archive, not a summary of a summary.

What would change the answer: one solid-material test above 30% O₂, one partial-gravity solid burn, and a matched 1 g / µg matrix. All three are named on the site as missing observations, not as future features.
